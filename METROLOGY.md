# AncientScriptLab metrological standard

Version: 1.0

Status: Official project standard

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the metrological framework of AncientScriptLab.

AncientScriptLab is treated as a measurement system rather than only a software project.

The objective is to ensure that every numerical feature produced by the system is:

- mathematically defined,
- experimentally validated,
- reproducible,
- traceable to a certified reference corpus,
- accompanied by documented limits of applicability.

---

## 2. Fundamental principle

No algorithm may be considered scientifically valid until it has passed the complete validation and certification procedure defined in this document.

Validation is mandatory for:

- new algorithms,
- algorithm modifications,
- preprocessing changes,
- binary conversion changes,
- topology implementation changes,
- geometry implementation changes.

## 4. Measured topological quantities

The topology package measures:

- connected components,
- hole count,
- Euler characteristic,
- foreground area,
- component areas,
- area ratios,
- density measures.

Fundamental identity:

χ = C − H

This identity is experimentally verified on the certified reference corpus.

---

## 5. Validation protocol

Every topology algorithm must pass the following validation stages.

### V1. Euler correctness

Verification of connected components, holes, and Euler characteristic.

### V2. Area conservation

Verification of total area and component area consistency.

### V3. Density identity

Verification that density equals foreground area divided by image area.

### V4A. Translation invariance

Verification under safe image translations.

### V5. Rotation invariance

Verification under rotations.

### V5B. Reflection invariance

Verification under mirror transformations.

### V6C. Resolution stability

Measurement of topological stability under controlled resolution degradation.

Future stages:

- V7. Noise metrology,
- V8. Threshold stability,
- V9. Morphological stability,
- V10. Cross-implementation consistency.

---

## 6. Certified invariances

The topology package is certified to be invariant to:

- translation,
- rotation,
- reflection.

Scale invariance is not required.

Instead, resolution stability is measured experimentally.

---

## 7. Resolution stability

Topology is evaluated only within documented resolution limits.

Experimental measurements demonstrate stability of the certified reference configurations down to 50% resolution under controlled degradation.

Critical resolution thresholds are measured rather than assumed.

---

---

## 9. Reference corpus modification policy

Any modification of the reference corpus requires complete re-certification.

Certification is successful only if all reference objects match their mathematical ground truth.

Failure of certification invalidates the corpus certification status.

---

## 10. Measurement traceability

Every numerical result produced by AncientScriptLab must be traceable to:

- reference corpus version,
- certification report,
- algorithm version,
- preprocessing version,
- FeatureContext version,
- execution engine version.

Traceability is mandatory for all scientific reports.

---

## 11. Metrological changes

A metrological change is any modification capable of altering numerical measurement results.

Examples:

- binary threshold changes,
- connectivity changes,
- morphological processing changes,
- interpolation changes,
- FeatureContext changes,
- OpenCV version changes.

Any metrological change requires complete re-certification.

---

## 12. Scientific reporting requirements

All published numerical results must include:

- algorithm version,
- corpus version,
- preprocessing version,
- validation status,
- certification status,
- applicable resolution limits.

---

## 13. Mandatory rule

A numerical result produced by AncientScriptLab is considered scientifically admissible only if generated by a certified algorithm operating within certified metrological conditions.

---

## 14. Human interpretation vs mathematical topology

Visual appearance is not a valid substitute for mathematical topology.

Ground truth must be established through:

1. geometric specification,
2. mathematical topological interpretation,
3. independent experimental certification.

The Reference Corpus certification procedure has demonstrated that visual intuition may differ from mathematical topological structure.

Topological truth is determined by certified measurement, not by visual inspection.

---

## 15. Experimental status of noise metrology

Noise metrology is currently under experimental investigation and is **not part of the certified measurement pipeline**.

Experimental studies of salt-and-pepper noise, resolution degradation, and observation uncertainty are maintained for research purposes only.

The certified topology algorithms and the certified Reference Corpus remain independent of the experimental noise models.

Experimental noise models will be integrated into the certified measurement framework **only if analysis of real corpora demonstrates reproducible anomalies attributable to observation noise**.

Until such evidence is established, noise metrology shall remain an experimental research direction and shall not affect certified measurement results.
